From Microbots to YOLOv11 – My Internship Journey in Cell Culture & Microgel Counting

2025-05-29

microroboticscomputer visioncell cultureyolo

I started my master’s because I want to contribute to the cure of neurodegenerative diseases like Parkinson’s and Alzheimer’s, and I’m planning to apply for a PhD in this field as well.

I’m especially fascinated by micro/nano-biorobotics and brain tissue or neural modeling as tools for fundamental brain treatment.

That’s why I joined the MBRL lab as an intern - to gain hands-on experience with cell culture and to learn how nanorobots can actually be built.

At first, I was more interested in nanoparticles, but while writing my research proposal here, I realized how broad the field of microrobotics actually is:

  • Design
  • Actuation
  • Locomotion
  • Planning
  • Tracking
  • Navigation

After analyzing four papers recommended by my professor, I found myself most drawn to Design and Navigation.

Eventually, I wrote a proposal focused on “Dopamine-Responsive Aptamer Nanorobots for On-Demand and Targeted Levodopa Delivery in Parkinson’s Disease.”

Through this process, I became particularly interested in working with BBB (blood-brain barrier) and CMOS-based microrobots.

For example, I’m convinced that within the next decade, microrobots will inevitably evolve into self-controlled systems with built-in CMOS.

And with continuous in vivo experiments, I also believe that there will be ways to bypass the BBB’s tight junctions and deliver drugs exactly where they’re needed.

Alongside the proposal and learning cell culture (including passaging), I was given a very practical task - something that could actually contribute to the PhD students’ projects in the lab: Count the number of microgels and cells in these images.

After refining the task with my supervising PhD student, the rules were set:

  • Overlapping microgels and the cells inside them are not counted.
  • Microgels cut off at the image edge with more than 50% outside are excluded.
  • Cells are counted even if they overlap.
  • Cells outside of any microgel are not counted.
  • The output should be a single Excel file (.xlsx) with two sheets:
    • microgel_id_cell_count – microgel ID and number of cells inside
    • microgel_id_cell_id – microgel ID (duplicates allowed) and IDs of cells inside each microgel

Since I had already taken AI classes, my first instinct was: wouldn’t segmentation be more accurate?

Especially since there’s already a famous tool called CellPose.

And indeed, using CellPose I was able to detect microgels… but then the problems started:

  1. The pre-trained model was hard to customize.
  2. Performance varied a lot by image - sometimes great, sometimes useless.
  3. With images full of microgels, it took over 5 minutes per image. (As a Korean, this was unacceptable, way too slow 😂)
  4. Cells inside microgels were barely detected. I even tried a naive approach — count “round” shapes inside microgels - but cells aren’t perfect circles, and overlaps broke the method completely.
  5. I also considered cropping each microgel into a separate image and then running CellPose… but just counting the microgels already overloaded the server. I literally had to rotate through 4–5 Google accounts just to keep using TPUs.

→ Segmentation was out.

So I turned to YOLOv11 and object detection.

Problem: no dataset.

Solution: make one.

I hand-labeled 49 images. It took me 8 hours straight 😂

The model trained, but of course, with only 49 images, it still missed new variations and unfamiliar image types.

Still, YOLO was fast, and with a bit of post-processing rules (and some human correction), it was actually practical.

So, I built a website.

  • Upload an image → YOLO model counts microgels and cells
  • Human-in-the-loop → check results, draw or delete boxes if needed
  • Get accurate counts of only the microgels/cells you want
  • Export results directly to Excel
  • Plus: export YOLO training .txt labels → quickly refine datasets for future training

In short, I now had a pipeline to get labeling done much faster and generate usable datasets for improving the model. And yes… I built the whole website in a single day. 😎
